# ADR-011: Multilingual retrieval in alternative_finder


## Context

`supplier_documents` was, until this work, 100% English regardless of
supplier country — even for suppliers in Mexico, Colombia, China,
Vietnam, and Egypt. This meant the demo's "hybrid search + reranking"
capability was never actually exercised against non-English source
material, and any claim of language-agnostic retrieval was untested.

The goal was to make a defensible, measured claim: that
`alternative_finder` finds and correctly audits real supplier evidence
regardless of the language it's written in — not just in English.

## Decision

### 1. Scope: 4 languages, chosen by failure mode, not by country count

We selected Spanish, Chinese (simplified), Vietnamese, and Arabic —
not to maximize country coverage, but because each stresses a
different failure mode of the retrieval pipeline:

- **Spanish** — control/baseline. Already worked reasonably well
  (shared Latin script, cognates, proper nouns survive translation).
- **Chinese** — no whitespace word boundaries; stresses tokenization
  (lexical search over-matches on a per-ideogram basis).
- **Vietnamese** — Latin script with spaces, but near-zero lexical
  overlap with English; stresses the "looks easy, isn't" case.
- **Arabic** — the only RTL script in scope; stresses presentation
  (bidi/text-direction), not just retrieval. Also has unrelated
  tokenization behavior from CJK (see Finding 3 below).

### 2. Root-cause diagnosis before any fix

Root cause: the search query (`profile_text`, generated by the Plan
node) is always synthesized in English, regardless of the target
document's language. `$rankFusion` combines a vector arm (weight 0.7)
and a lexical arm (weight 0.3, `$search` on `lucene.standard`). With
an English query against a non-English document:

- The lexical arm frequently returns **zero results** for the
  document (no token overlap) — not a low score, an absence.
- RRF math (`k=60`) imposes a **hard ceiling** on any document absent
  from one arm: `score = weight_present / (60 + rank)`. A document
  present in both arms at rank ≤27 beats a document that is #1 in the
  vector arm but missing from the lexical arm. This is a structural
  property of the fusion formula, not a quality problem with the
  embedding model.

Confirmed by measurement (weight sweep across `{vector, text}` ratios
summing to 1): no weight combination rescues the worst case (Chinese),
because its deficit is *in* the vector arm itself (rank #8 of 50),
which the fusion formula cannot fix. Vietnamese is only rescued by
effectively disabling the lexical arm (`text: 0.05`), which sacrifices
exact-term search for every other document. **Adjusting `$rankFusion`
weights was ruled out** as a fix — the cost (breaking exact
certificate-number search) exceeds the benefit, and it doesn't even
solve the hardest case.

### 3. The reranker resolves it — measured, not assumed

The `$rerank` stage reads raw chunk text directly, independent of the
Lucene analyzer and the RRF ceiling. Measured with the same-model
external fallback (Tier 2 — native `$rerank` is unavailable on this
deployment, see ADR-007 addendum):

| Language | Without reranker | With reranker |
|---|---|---|
| Chinese | #34 of 50, 20 suppliers ahead → excluded from top-5 | #4 of 50, 3 ahead → **included** |
| Vietnamese | #28 of 50, 16 ahead → excluded | #3 of 50, 2 ahead → **included** |
| Arabic (2 docs) | #23–26 of 50, 15–16 ahead → excluded | #2 of 50, 1 ahead → **included** |
| Spanish | #4 of 48 → already included | #2 of 48 → improved |

**4 of 4 languages resolved with a reranker available (native or
fallback).** No change to `$rankFusion` weights, no per-language
analyzer, and no change to `profile_text` generation was needed. The
originally planned "Layer 0 generates `profile_text` in the target
document's language" work (a larger, riskier change touching the Plan
node and the search index) was **not implemented**, because it solves
a problem the reranker already solves more simply.

### 4. A secondary finding: Chinese and Arabic fail for opposite reasons

Not previously known. Both stem from the same root cause — no
per-language analyzer — but manifest oppositely:

- **Chinese**: no word boundaries → UAX#29 tokenizes per ideogram →
  high recall, low precision (the lexical arm returns noise —
  unrelated certificates from other countries matching on shared
  numeric/Latin tokens).
- **Arabic**: proper word boundaries, but no morphological
  normalization (definite article `ال`, alef variants, tāʾ marbūṭa)
  → high precision, brittle recall (exact spelling match or nothing —
  a single definite article changes the lexical score by 6×).

This doesn't need a code fix (the reranker covers it), but if the
lexical arm's Arabic precision/recall is ever needed for its own
sake, the fix is a `multi`-analyzer entry (`lucene.arabic`) on the
`chunk_text` field — a single index, no fragmentation.

### 5. Real content: 12 of 30 candidate chunks qualified — not a target number

We reviewed 30 non-contract chunks across 17 candidate suppliers
(9 real Spanish-speaking suppliers + 8 China/Vietnam/Egypt suppliers)
against a language criterion: **language depends on who authored the
document in real life, not the supplier's nationality.**

- `certificate` / `sustainability_report`: local language if the
  issuing body is a genuinely local entity, or an international
  certifier's *local branch, identified by country in the text*
  (e.g. "Bureau Veritas Mexico", "SGS (China) Co., Ltd.") — added as
  an explicit, documented exception to the original criterion, since
  a bare international name with no country qualifier is realistically
  English/bilingual for an exporting supplier.
- `email`: excluded — 100% of reviewed emails are addressed to
  `procurement@freshmart.com`. This is buyer-facing correspondence,
  the same case as a contract, not internal correspondence.
- `audit_report`: excluded — none name a third-party auditor; the
  language reads as FreshMart's own risk notes, not a supplier
  document.
- `contract`: excluded per the original rule (unchanged).

Result: **12 chunks converted** (8 Spanish across 5 suppliers, 4
showcase: 1 Chinese, 2 Vietnamese, 1 Arabic). This is smaller than
initially expected — most certificates in this corpus name
multinational certifiers without a country qualifier, and no email
or audit document in the reviewed set had a genuine basis for
non-English content. This is flagged as a **content gap in the seed**,
not a design decision (see "Known limitations" below).

### 6. `language` field

Added as BCP-47 (`es-MX`, `es-CO`, `es-ES`, `zh-Hans`, `vi`, `ar`, and
`en` for the untranslated majority) to **all 146** `supplier_documents`
chunks — not just the 12 translated ones. Rationale: an absent field
on ~92% of documents would make "missing `language`" an implicit,
fragile synonym for "English" throughout the application layer;
an explicit field on every document is more robust for any future
filtering or analytics.

`citation.language` was *not* propagated to `supplier_alternatives` in
this pass — the retrieved excerpt already carries its language
implicitly, and no frontend need for it surfaced once the RTL fix (see
below) turned out not to require it (`dir="auto"` needs no language
hint).

### 7. Frontend: RTL support, minimal scope

Diagnosed and implemented independently of the retrieval work:
`dir="auto"` on the citation excerpt, email body, and header-value
elements (`CitationEvidence.jsx`, `LogDrawer.jsx`), plus removal of a
synthetic italic style that deforms Arabic glyphs. Full layout
mirroring (physical→logical CSS properties, LeafyGreen component
internals, Bootstrap RTL stylesheet) was explicitly ruled out — the
application chrome is English-language UI; mirroring the whole layout
for an RTL excerpt would be incorrect, not just unnecessary. Embedded
Latin dates/identifiers within RTL text can still visually reorder
(bidi-neutral separators); fixing this needs `<bdi>` wrapping around
parsed identifiers, out of scope for this pass.

### 8. Rerank fallback (see ADR-007 addendum)

Native `$rerank` is unavailable in this environment (Preview feature,
deployment-dependent rollout) — confirmed via isolated testing,
feature-flag enumeration, and a support ticket
([ISTTH-874](https://jira.mongodb.org/browse/ISTTH-874)). A three-tier
fallback (native → external Voyage API → unranked `$rankFusion` order)
keeps the pipeline resilient regardless of environment, and is the
mechanism that made the measurements in Section 3 possible at all in
this deployment.

## Consequences

- Multilingual retrieval works today, in production, verified
  end-to-end (not just against synthetic test chunks) — confirmed by
  a real evaluation run where both a Spanish `sustainability_report`
  and a Vietnamese `certificate` were retrieved, correctly audited
  (LLM Audit node read and reasoned over the Vietnamese text without
  degrading it to `unknown`), and surfaced in the final shortlist.
- This depends on a reranker being available (native or fallback).
  If both the native stage and the external fallback become
  unavailable simultaneously, non-English documents revert to the
  pre-fix behavior (excluded from most top-5 shortlists).
- The external fallback (Tier 2) sends chunk text to a third-party API
  when active — a real, documented exception to the "no data leaves
  Atlas" claim made elsewhere in this project's README, scoped
  explicitly as a resilience fallback, not the default path.

## Known limitations (flagged, not silently accepted)

- The Chinese showcase document is the shortest chunk in the corpus
  (128 chars, vs. 250 in English) — a byproduct of Chinese text
  density, not an error, but it reintroduces a length/language
  confound that earlier synthetic measurements could never fully
  separate.
- 4 of 9 real Spanish-speaking suppliers (Monterrey, Sinaloa,
  Medellín, Lima) have zero chunks that qualify for translation under
  the applied criterion — their certificates don't name an issuing
  body at all. Not a design decision; a content gap.
- No supplier document in the reviewed corpus is genuinely internal
  correspondence (supplier ↔ own team) — every email is buyer-facing.
  If local-language operational correspondence is wanted in a future
  demo, that document type doesn't exist yet in the model.
- Arabic lexical search precision/recall (`lucene.arabic`) was
  diagnosed but not implemented — not needed while a reranker is
  available, but noted for future work if the lexical arm's Arabic
  behavior is ever load-bearing on its own.
- The Audit node's prompt has no explicit language-independence
  instruction. In the one real run observed, non-English evidence was
  audited correctly — but the guardrail against a false `unknown`
  degradation (as opposed to a false `compliant` upgrade, which *is*
  guarded deterministically) does not exist in code. Flagged as
  future work, not resolved here.

## Recommendation for future data generation

If the corpus is enriched again with coherence/richness in mind:
- Ensure at least one certificate/audit per real supplier names a
  genuinely local body or a local branch with an explicit country
  qualifier (today: Monterrey, Sinaloa, Medellín, Lima have none).
- Introduce a genuinely internal email document type (supplier ↔ own
  team), distinct from buyer-facing correspondence — the only way
  local-language `email` content can appear without forcing a
  translation of correspondence with an English-speaking buyer.
- If the zh/vi/ar showcase should extend beyond the current 4
  documents, add suppliers or documents in those countries that name
  a locally-identified certifying/auditing body — today Shenzhen,
  Shanghai, Dongguan, and Binh Duong have none.
